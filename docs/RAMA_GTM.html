RAMA · Go-to-market plan

Middle-market fund GPs · freemium agent inside the portfolio-management platforms they already use · subscription once traction gates are met · 17 October 2026
In one paragraph. RAMA enters the middle market as a free agent that plugs into the portfolio-monitoring platform a GP already runs (Chronograph, iLEVEL, Allvue, Cobalt, Dynamo, eFront) and the accounting exports its portfolio companies already produce. The free tier closes one fund's portfolio companies every month, verifies every figure against its source, and writes guarded risk cards into the GP's own platform. Every run is traced, so usage and usability are measured, not guessed. The product iterates on that evidence. When five pre-set traction gates are met, the paid tiers switch on: a monthly or annual subscription priced per portfolio company under coverage, sold to the fund CFO, with investor relations as the second buyer. Revenue is not the goal of the free period; a product that GPs keep using is.

1. Who we sell to, and why the middle market

The buyer: the fund CFO or controller of a middle-market private credit or private equity GP, roughly $300M to $3B under management, running two to six funds, with 10 to 60 portfolio companies or borrowers, and either an in-house close or a thin fund administrator. The daily user: the fund accountant or analyst who runs the monthly close and the portfolio-monitoring platform. The second buyer (paid tier): investor relations, who answer the LP's questions.

Why this segment first. Large managers (eFront, iLEVEL enterprise) buy on multi-year procurement cycles with vendor-security reviews we can't yet pass; emerging managers under $300M have one accountant and little budget. The middle market has the pain (a real close, real LPs asking real questions) and buys fast, and it already pays for a monitoring platform, so our agent arrives as an addition, not a replacement. There is no public count of middle-market GPs; sizing comes from the SEC's Form ADV filings and PitchBook's firm data, which we pull in the first week (section 9).

The problem, quantified (sources in the judges' script): $1.7–2 trillion of private credit; a record 6.0% U.S. default rate, more than half through interest deferrals and PIK; LPs seeing the quarter 60–90 days after it ends. The number that warns an LP sits in a borrower's books, and nobody connects it to the market in time.

5. The traction gates: when the subscription switches on

The paid tiers launch when all five gates are met, measured over a trailing 60 days. Announced to every free user at sign-up, so the switch is expected, not a surprise.

If a gate isn't met by month 9, we don't launch pricing; we publish what we learned and fix the product. A free tier that nobody uses monthly is a verdict, not a stage.

AdministratorMulti-GP tenancy, white-label notes, API, SLANegotiated; floor $25,000 per year per administrator plus per-company feesFund administrators (section 8)

Why per company per month. It is the unit the CFO already thinks in (the close is per company), it grows with the portfolio rather than with seats, and it maps directly to our cost (one close, a few model calls). At 20 companies, Close is $9,000 a month; a single fund accountant costs more. Why the annual option: the close is a monthly habit, so annual billing matches a year of closes and funds our connectors. Price discovery: gate 5 includes ten written willingness-to-pay conversations; the numbers above are the opening position, and the final list price is set from those conversations, not from this document.

What is never charged for: citations, guardrails, the audit trail, or the LP's own threshold settings. Charging for the controls would make the free tier unsafe and the paid tier a bribe.

8. Channels: how GPs find it

  • Platform marketplaces and partner pages (primary): Allvue, iLEVEL and Chronograph each list partner integrations; being the "risk notes from verified closes" partner is the cheapest distribution there is, and the free tier is what gets us listed. Ask: a listing, a joint webinar, a shared design partner.
  • Fund administrators (the multiplier): one administrator closes books for dozens of GPs. Target the middle-market specialists (Gen II, Standish, Alter Domus's mid-market practice, Juniper Square's fund-admin arm) with the Administrator tier: they keep the client, we remove their close bottleneck. Start with one, as a design partner, in phase 2.
  • The CFO network: private-fund CFO roundtables and the PEI CFO & COO Forum; a 20-minute talk titled "The number in the borrower's books that warns the LP" with the PIK story. No ads.
  • Public evidence: the open repo, the evaluation results (72 cases, 59 → 72), the PRISM traces, and a monthly "what the agents got wrong" note. Trust in this market comes from showing the failures, not hiding them.
  • Nicrron and PRISM: both partners have a reason to feature a customer that proves their tools on real finance work; ask each for a case study and a listing.
  • LP pull (later): an LP that sees a RAMA note in one GP's reporting asks its other GPs for the same. The destination label stays honest until an LP-side integration exists.

10. Risks and the honest answers

Platforms build this themselvesThey may; our edge is the verified close underneath the note, which a monitoring platform doesn't do. Stay the partner that makes their data trustworthy, and keep the engine open-source-visible so the trust is inspectable.
"Is this investment advice?"Descriptive only, measured against the LP's own thresholds, no transaction-linked fees, a classifier and blocklist on every card, hand-off text for "what should I do?". Securities counsel reviews the card design before the LP Insight tier launches.

11. What we will not do

  • Charge for safety: citations, guardrails, audit trail and thresholds stay free in every tier.
  • Claim accuracy we haven't measured: every published rate is tier B or C (blind or real), never a fixture with its answers opened.
  • Build a second ledger, resell market data, or write numbers into a customer's platform.
  • Take write access to a real ledger without written authorisation.
  • Say "Chronograph integration" until one exists; the demonstration label stays until then.
RAMA provides risk analytics, not investment advice. Prices and thresholds in this plan are proposals to be set from the design-partner conversations. Market figures are sourced in the judges' script; the segment count is to be measured from Form ADV and PitchBook in week 1.
